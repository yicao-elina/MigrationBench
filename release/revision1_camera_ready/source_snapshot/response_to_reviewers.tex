\section*{Response to Reviewer 1}

We thank Reviewer~1 for the careful and technically detailed assessment. The comments led us to audit the underlying calculations, to state each claim at the strength the data support, and to strengthen the code release. The principal changes are the following.

\begin{itemize}
    \item The study is presented as a task-conditioned benchmark on one system, Cr-doped \ce{Sb2Te3}, with the MACE model family; claims of a universal ranking or a generalizable standard are removed (A4).
    \item Fixed-path NEB scoring and self-relaxed NEB are separate operators. Fixed-path scoring compares model energy differences on common DFT images, and self-relaxed NEB tests whether a model's own forces generate a stable band. The 0.41~eV value of the earlier SI Fig.~S3 belonged to a different path and is withdrawn (A1).
    \item The split is trajectory-grouped, with SOAP/RMSD overlap checks against the 25 NEB images, and FT-600K, Scratch, and a Scratch-5\% control are retrained with three independent initializations (A2, A3, A5, C10).
    \item The asymmetric short MD comparison is replaced by a seed-aware one-nanosecond benchmark. FT-600K and FT-Multi$_T$ are summarized across training seeds, foundation representatives are summarized by model-to-model spread, and from-scratch branches are retained as stability failures. No same-campaign heat-flux record is available, so HFACF, Green--Kubo thermal conductivity, $\kappa$, and transport ranking are not reported (C9).
    \item Projection and SHAP results are restated at the strength of the evidence, with original-space silhouette scores and cross-validated surrogate fidelity (A6, C11, C13).
    \item The zero-force exporters and the inactive feature channel are corrected, and the code release is populated (C8, C12, C13).
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Overall response to Reviewer 1's summary assessment}

We agree that this study should be presented as a task-conditioned case study rather than as evidence for a universal ordering of specialist and foundation MLIPs. We have therefore organized the benchmark around two linked axes: the configuration distribution moves from near-equilibrium structures to migration and collective-displacement probes, while the measurement operator moves from common-reference scoring to model-generated paths and trajectories.

The first level is a static single-point baseline for the trained variants: energy and force RMSEs on DFT test configurations (Table~S1 and Fig.~S10). The second level consists of equilibrium and near-equilibrium MD tests: RDFs, temperature and pressure traces, VACF, and MSD at 600~K (Fig.~2 and Supplementary Figs.~S2, S11, and S12). All four routes reproduce the principal local coordination features, and the zero-shot foundation model shows a persistent pressure offset.

The third level is fixed-path NEB evaluation: QE defines an image set, and each MLIP is evaluated on the same fixed images. On the within-gap path, the grouped three-seed absolute fixed-path reference errors are $0.47\pm0.12$~eV for FT-600K, $1.20\pm0.27$~eV for Scratch, and $0.68\pm0.20$~eV for the Scratch-5\% control. Four representative foundation checkpoints assign 1.50, 1.79, 1.60, and 1.84~eV to the same images against the QE image-set reference of 0.34~eV, so all four exceed that reference by 1.17--1.50~eV. The path is marked UNCONVERGED in the current SI status table, so these values are fixed-image references rather than physical migration barriers. On Q1--Q3, FT-600K fixed-path errors are about 10~eV in every initialization.

The fourth level is self-relaxed NEB, in which the MLIP generates its own pathway through model-in-the-loop force feedback. The Scratch and fine-tuned models do not converge a band under this protocol, so self-relaxed NEB serves as a stability test. Collective interlayer sliding (Fig.~4) adds a nonlocal probe in which fine-tuning that improves local migration chemistry does not preserve long-range registry.

Taken together, the results support a conditional interpretation. Fine-tuning improves the within-gap observable under this protocol, while the foundation checkpoints remain miscalibrated on that path and the adapted models do not transfer uniformly to larger distortions or collective sliding. The conclusions refer to this host, dopant, and model family, and this scope is stated in the title, abstract, Introduction, Figure~1, Results, and Supplementary Information.

\subsection*{A1. Fig.~3d versus Fig.~S3: NEB barrier discrepancy}

\noindent\textbf{Reviewer comment.}
\begin{quote}
\itshape
``Reconcile the foundation-model in-gap barrier discrepancy: state explicitly whether Fig. 3d (fixed-geometry, $\sim$0.7 eV overestimate, `unacceptable') and Fig. S3 (self-run NEB, 0.41 eV, `exceptional') use the same pathway and DFT reference; fix the reference value (0.34 vs.\ $\sim$0.3 eV); present both evaluations side-by-side and explain why relaxation changes the verdict. As written, the paper's central ranking is ambiguous.''
\end{quote}

\noindent\textbf{Response.}
The two figures use different paths and different operators, and the original manuscript did not say so. We have separated them.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Fixed-path NEB evaluation (main-text Fig.~3a).}
    The DFT-relaxed NEB images are held fixed, and each MLIP evaluates the energies of those geometries. This tests energetic calibration on a common DFT-defined migration path. The path is the 61-atom, one-Cr system $\mathrm{CrSb_{24}Te_{36}}$ with five DFT-relaxed images; it is a within-gap, interpolative probe.
    \begin{equation}
    \Delta E^{\mathrm{fixed}}_M(\Gamma_{\mathrm{DFT}})
    =
    \max_i E_M(X_i^{\mathrm{DFT}})
    -
    E_M(X_0^{\mathrm{DFT}}).
    \end{equation}

    \item \textbf{Self-relaxed NEB evaluation.}
    The MLIP starts from the endpoint structures and relaxes its own elastic band with its own forces:
    \begin{equation}
    \Gamma_M^\star = \operatorname{NEB}_M(X_0,X_1),
    \qquad
    \Delta E^{\mathrm{self}}_M
    =
    \max_i E_M(X_i^{M,\star})
    -
    E_M(X_0).
    \end{equation}
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Reference value.} The QE forward-minus-first-image energy difference for the within-gap path G1 (path names are defined in SI Table~S7) is 0.336~eV, which is 0.34~eV at manuscript precision; the reviewer's ``$\sim$0.3~eV'' is the same value at one significant figure. G1 is marked UNCONVERGED in the current SI status table, so this value is used as a fixed-image QE reference rather than a converged physical barrier. Fixed-path errors compare MLIP and QE energies on the same five images.

\textbf{The 0.41~eV value.} The earlier SI Fig.~S3 value of 0.41~eV does not belong to this path. The audit shows that it comes from the 82-atom, two-Cr system $\mathrm{Cr_2Sb_{32}Te_{48}}$ with twelve images, that the endpoint and internal-image calculators differed, and that the final band has a serialization defect. Uniform rescoring of the preserved band gives a forward endpoint-referenced value of 0.00~eV, a reverse value of 0.38~eV, and a whole-band span of 1.04~eV, not 0.41~eV. We have withdrawn the value, and we make no statement that relaxation changes the ranking of the models.

\textbf{Side-by-side presentation.} The revised SI figure (Fig.~S15) puts the two operators next to each other: panel (a) shows fixed-path energy differences on the within-gap path, and panel (b) shows the self-relaxed NEB of the Scratch model, whose band does not converge (maximum image energy $10^{2}$--$10^{4}$~eV above the endpoint). The fixed-image comparison is therefore kept separate from self-relaxed path stability. A DFT-rescored, same-system self-relaxed physical-barrier comparison would be a separate study and lies outside the scope of this one.

As an additional matched fixed-path check, we evaluated ten Foundation checkpoints (MP-0b3, MPA0, OMAT-0 small/medium, MH-0/MH-1, Agnesi medium, and three Agnesi stress/density variants) on the same five within-gap images. MP-0b3, MPA0, OMAT-0 medium, and MH-1 gave forward-minus-first-image energy differences of 1.50, 1.79, 1.60, and 1.84~eV against the 0.34~eV QE image-set reference, so their absolute errors range from 1.17 to 1.50~eV, and MPA0 is not the most accurate. The G1 image set is marked UNCONVERGED in the SI status table, so these are fixed-image comparisons rather than physical migration barriers. The ten individual profiles and the family-aggregated profiles are in the SI (the family bands show sub-model variation, not seed uncertainty). The preflight manifest, raw profiles, and SHA256 ledger are released with the revision artifacts.

\subsection*{A2. Train/test overlap for FT-600K}

\noindent\textbf{Reviewer comment.}
\begin{quote}
\itshape
``Rule out train/test overlap for FT-600K: report the minimum SOAP-distance (or RMSD) between each in-gap NEB image and its nearest fine-tuning frame, or re-run FT-600K with the NEB-adjacent trajectory segment excluded, and confirm the 0.16 eV result survives.''
\end{quote}

\noindent\textbf{Response.}
We agree that the original frame-level shuffle did not rule out trajectory-adjacent overlap. We rebuilt the split by source-trajectory provenance and re-evaluated the fine-tuning files. Every source trajectory is assigned in full to train, validation, or test, which gives 1,786/311/267 configurations for the full grouped split. The Scratch-5\% control uses the same validation and test partitions and an 89-configuration grouped training subset. The overlap check covers 25 fixed NEB images spanning the within-gap path and the gap-to-QL/into-QL paths.

The check finds zero exact global matches, zero exact Cr-local matches, and zero Cr-local RMSD values at or below 0.05~\AA{} in both training splits. The minimum training-set SOAP cosine distances are $5.4\times10^{-4}$ for the full grouped split and $6.4\times10^{-4}$ for Scratch-5\%, and the minimum comparable Cr-local RMSDs are 2.06~\AA{} for both; all lie above the near-duplicate thresholds (SOAP $\le10^{-4}$, RMSD $\le0.05$~\AA{}). SOAP is evaluated for all target images, and the RMSD comparison covers species-composition-compatible local environments (two comparable training candidates for the full split and one for Scratch-5\%). No fixed-path image has an exact, SOAP, or RMSD near-duplicate in the fine-tuning files.

We then retrained and evaluated FT-600K from the OMAT-FT checkpoint, Scratch, and Scratch-5\% under the grouped protocol with three independent initializations. On the within-gap path, relative to the 0.336~eV QE image-set reference, the absolute fixed-path reference errors are $0.47\pm0.12$~eV for FT-600K (OMAT-FT start), $1.20\pm0.27$~eV for Scratch, and $0.68\pm0.20$~eV for Scratch-5\%. The single-checkpoint MACE-MPA-0 comparator gives 0.24~eV. The 0.16~eV value from the original single-run, frame-level evaluation is therefore not reproduced under grouped splitting, and the grouped three-seed value of $0.47\pm0.12$~eV replaces it as the FT-600K result. The other evaluated paths are reported per path in the SI; they are not pooled into the within-gap result because they belong to different path classes. FT-Multi$_T$ is evaluated on the frame-level baseline and in the MD and sliding tests.

To make the distinction between static pointwise accuracy and process-conditioned configuration-space coverage explicit, we added two configuration-space panels (Fig.~\ref{fig:task_conditioned_panel_a} and Fig.~\ref{fig:task_conditioned_panel_c}), a four-model validation-RMSE history (Fig.~\ref{fig:original_training_rmse_four_model}), and a stage-resolved failure diagnostic (Fig.~\ref{fig:failure_stage_grouped}). The reference panel uses a PCA fit on grouped training data only and shows the complete 1,786/311/267 split. The stored MLIP-generated 600~K MD rows comprise Foundation 4,961 rows with 3 SOAP-valid frames and 4,958 short-contact rows, Scratch 4,001 rows with 1,310 valid and 2,691 short-contact rows, FT-600K 4,601 valid rows, and FT-Multi$_T$ 4,001 valid rows. The four-model RMSE figure reconstructs 87 original default-head validation checkpoints from the training logs.

\subsection*{A3. Uncertainty in kinetic results}

\noindent\textbf{Reviewer comment.}
\begin{quote}
\itshape
``Add uncertainty to the kinetic results: retrain at least Scratch and FT-600K with $\geq$3 seeds and report barrier-error mean +/- std (the 3-seed protocol already exists for Table S1). This simultaneously tests the `chance' explanation for the scratch model's deep-penetration result---if the low error is seed-stable, `chance' is refuted; if seed-dependent, it is supported. Either way, replace the current unfalsifiable assertion with evidence.''
\end{quote}

\noindent\textbf{Response.}
We agree. We retrained Scratch and FT-600K, together with the Scratch-5\% control, under the grouped protocol with three independent training initializations and report the mean and sample standard deviation across runs. On the within-gap path the absolute fixed-path errors are $0.47\pm0.12$~eV for FT-600K, $1.20\pm0.27$~eV for Scratch, and $0.68\pm0.20$~eV for Scratch-5\%.

The same grouped files test the chance explanation on the three gap-to-QL/into-QL paths (Q1, Q2, and Q3). The Scratch errors are $0.51\pm0.11$, $0.72\pm0.55$, and $1.77\pm1.00$~eV, and the FT-600K errors are $9.60\pm0.57$, $10.40\pm0.44$, and $9.70\pm0.20$~eV. Following the reviewer's criterion, the low Scratch error on Q1 is seed-stable (standard deviation 0.11~eV), so it is not a chance outcome; on Q2 and Q3 the Scratch errors vary strongly between runs (standard deviations 0.55 and 1.00~eV), so the Scratch result on those images depends on the initialization. FT-600K deviates by about 10~eV on all three paths in every initialization, so seed variation does not account for its failure on these images. We have replaced the phrase ``by chance'' with these statements.

These errors are fixed-path errors on identical images, measured against the QE image-set reference of each path; for paths marked UNCONVERGED, the reference is an image-set span rather than a physical migration barrier. The standard deviations are sample standard deviations across training seeds. Run-level values are in the SI table and raw CSV.

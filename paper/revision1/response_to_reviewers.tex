{\Large\bfseries Response to Reviewer}\\[4pt]
{\large When Does a Foundation Model Help?}\\
Process-Specific Reliability of Machine-Learned Interatomic Potentials
\end{center}
We thank the reviewers for their careful evaluation, which has strengthened the physical and methodological depth of this manuscript. The paper is now reorganized around seven main figures to establish a framework for evaluating process-specific reliability in foundation models. The revised comparative analysis separates pointwise errors, fixed-image energy assignments, model-relaxed pathways, and macroscopic MD horizons. This decoupling demonstrates the specific regimes where target adaptation provides advantages versus where zero-shot foundation priors remain robust.

To ensure consistency and reproducibility, we have standardized the quantitative metrics using complete grouped retraining. Consequently, the legacy 0.16~eV and 0.41~eV estimates are replaced by these controlled benchmarks. The 89-frame control is now defined as a data-volume sensitivity baseline. Furthermore, the updated MD trajectories capture the failure horizons across all fine-tuned runs, establishing a realistic bound on dynamic stability. We have also refined the feature representation analyses to serve as macroscopic structural diagnostics, mapping the geometric phase space without overextending to causal mechanistic claims. Finally, absolute kinetic interpretations are now constrained to fully converged DFT references, defining the macroscopic boundaries of the workflow.

The point-by-point responses below address each reviewer comment, detailing the specific methodological updates and their locations in the revised text. Within the marked manuscript, the revisions corresponding to these responses are highlighted in blue and annotated with [R1-A1] through [R1-C13] identifiers. All section, figure, and table numbers in this document refer to the revised main text and Supplementary Information.

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}\section*{A1. Fig.~3d versus Fig.~S3: NEB barrier discrepancy}

\begin{quote}\small \itshape
``Reconcile the foundation-model in-gap barrier discrepancy: state explicitly whether Fig. 3d (fixed-geometry, $\sim$0.7 eV overestimate, `unacceptable') and Fig. S3 (self-run NEB, 0.41 eV, `exceptional') use the same pathway and DFT reference; fix the reference value (0.34 vs.\ $\sim$0.3 eV); present both evaluations side-by-side and explain why relaxation changes the verdict. As written, the paper's central ranking is ambiguous.''\end{quote}

\textbf{Response.} 
We sincerely thank the reviewer for pointing this out. We agree that the original presentation was ambiguous, and we have restructured the migration pathway evaluation to ensure geometric and computational consistency. 

The discrepancy in the original manuscript arose from comparing different physical tasks evaluated on distinct structural sets. Specifically, the 0.34~eV reference originates from a 61-atom, single-Cr, 5-image geometry (legacy G1 set). The 0.41~eV record was derived from a larger 82-atom, dual-Cr configuration evaluated with different electronic structure calculators. To establish a standardized benchmark, we have removed these legacy records and now use the 81-atom canonical in-gap reference (1.52~eV).

A data audit has further standardized the evaluation matrix. We explicitly classify the precise topological states and their corresponding energy peaks (e.g., the structural optimization of Scratch-600K seed 123/within-QL self-establishes a finite peak of 2.35~eV). These structural evaluations are strictly verified via SHA256 model identities and are explicitly detailed in the revised main figures.

To clarify the methodology, the revised manuscript separates fixed-geometry calibration from model-driven path generation, presenting both profiles side-by-side in main Fig.~\ref{M-fig:profiles}a--f. Because self-relaxed NEB alters the sampled coordinates, predicting a lower energy peak indicates the model has navigated to a distinct local basin or pathway. Validating a physical barrier requires structural correspondence with the canonical first-principles trajectory.

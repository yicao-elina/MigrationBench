\abstract{Universal machine-learned interatomic potentials (MLIPs) are emerging as foundation models for materials science, promising out-of-the-box atomistic simulations across diverse chemistries. 
Their practical value, however, depends on whether they reliably reproduce the specific physical processes required for an application. Using Cr-doped \ce{Sb2Te3} as a representative layered system featuring distinct interfacial and bulk environments, we compare specialist, zero-shot, and fine-tuned MLIPs across thermal configurations, dopant migration, and collective layer sliding.
Fixed-geometry energies, model-relaxed pathways, and molecular dynamics expose complementary failure modes. Target adaptation improves force accuracy and selected migration energies, yet fails to guarantee stable dynamics or reliable energies under strong distortions. Conversely, zero-shot checkpoints sustain longer trajectories while exhibiting calibration errors on specific migration configurations. Path relaxation can lower apparent barriers without recovering reference energy profiles. Structural projections and error surrogates characterize these differences without isolating their microscopic causes. These results establish a mandate for process-specific model selection: a useful potential must be tested both on configurations relevant to an observable and on those generated by its own forces. Foundation initialization is thus a resource to validate and adapt, rather than a guarantee of dynamical and thermodynamic reliability.}
\keywords{machine-learned interatomic potentials (MLIPs), foundation models, fine-tuning, migration, distribution shift, model validation}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
The advent of universal machine-learned interatomic potentials (MLIPs) is fundamentally shifting the paradigm of atomistic simulation. Rather than parameterizing a specialist potential for every new material, researchers can now initialize from a model trained across vast chemical spaces and either deploy it zero-shot or adapt it using a targeted reference dataset. MLIPs have made this prospect highly attractive by enabling first-principles accuracy at length and time scales inaccessible to density functional theory (DFT) alone~\citep{unke_machine_2021,bartok_gaussian_2010,ssmith_ani-1_2017,chen_accurate_2017,lindsey2017chimes,zhang_deep_2018,chmiela_sgdml_2017,batzner_e3-equivariant_2022,musaelian_learning_2023,zeng_deepmd-kit_2023,vandermause_active_2022,batatia_mace_nodate,wines_chips-ff_2025,friederich_machine-learned_2021}. Broadly trained universal potentials such as MACE-MP and CHGNet explicitly extend this capability across the periodic table~\citep{batatia_mace_foundation_2025,deng_chgnet_2023,chen_graph_2019,chen_universal_2022,yang_mattersim_2024,wood_uma_2025,merchant_scaling_2023}. The resulting choice is increasingly practical: should one deploy a universal checkpoint zero-shot, fine-tune it, or train a specialist from scratch?

For materials applications, the answer must ultimately be evaluated through the lens of specific physical processes. A model may reliably reproduce typical thermal fluctuations yet entirely misrepresent the short-lived coordination changes governing dopant motion. It may remain numerically stable while exploring a fundamentally inaccurate energy landscape. Conversely, minimal point-wise energy errors on a supplied transition path do not guarantee that the model's own generated forces will recover that pathway during a simulation. These distinctions are critical because activated motion is dictated by relative energies along rare transition configurations, whereas molecular dynamics (MD) autoregressively relies on the model's own force predictions to dictate subsequent phase-space exploration. Average error over a static test set and dynamical reliability in a target process are therefore fundamentally distinct metrics~\citep{fu2022forces,liu2023discrepancies,jacobs2025practical,schran_machine_2020,bernstein_quantifying_2019}. While broad materials benchmarks such as Matbench and JARVIS-Leaderboard assess property-prediction performance at scale, and generative benchmarks evaluate structural novelty, neither framework directly probes dynamical fidelity in complex, out-of-equilibrium physical processes~\citep{dunn_benchmarking_2020,choudhary2024jarvis,betala2025lemat}.

Recent work establishes both the promise and the conditional nature of transfer. \rev{A7}{Alghamdi \textit{et al.} show that zero-shot and lightly fine-tuned MACE models can reproduce Li diffusion observables in LiF; Bheemaguli \textit{et al.} distinguish migration-energy accuracy from transition-path geometry across battery materials; and Deng \textit{et al.} identify systematic softening of universal potentials on several out-of-distribution tasks~\citep{alghamdi2025maceLiF,bheemaguli2026mlipBarriers,deng2025softening}.} 
These studies make process-aware testing essential and indicate that adaptation success depends heavily on which regions of the energy landscape are constrained by target data~\citep{radova2025frozen_transfer,janet_designing_2019,lookman_active_2019,zuo_performance_2020,jinnouchi_phase_2020,zeni_data_2021,vandermause_on--fly_2020}. Yet, a critical question remains: how model performance shifts when evaluating the same material across fundamentally distinct physical processes and testing protocols.

Addressing this question requires an ideal model host that exposes a single chemical system to diverse, highly non-equivalent physical processes. We select Cr-doped \ce{Sb2Te3} as a representative benchmark system because its layered architecture natively decouples compositional complexity from multi-regime distribution shifts. Its quintuple layers comprise a Te--Sb--Te--Sb--Te stack separated by van der Waals gaps. A Cr atom migrating within the gap, approaching a layer boundary, or entering the interior encounters diverse coordination environments, whereas layer translation induces a collective registry change. This combination provides a compact set of physically distinct probes within a single host (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}a,c), decoupling process-specific distribution shifts from compositional changes. More broadly, layered 2D materials are widely investigated across spintronic, quantum, and energy applications, where stacking, intercalation, and magnetic doping serve as primary tuning levers~\citep{novoselov_2d_2016,manzeli_2d_2017,akinwande2017review,guo_stacking_2021,liu_roadmap_2024,he_topological_2022,qian_van_2024,song_electron_2018,nayak_microsoft_2025,jin_flexible_2019,gautam_creation_2024,whittingham_lithium_2004,winter_insertion_1998,hu_lithium_2024,gibertini_magnetic_2019,tokura_magnetic_2019}. Cr-doped \ce{Sb2Te3} represents an established platform for exploring the interplay between transition-metal doping and layered topological matter~\citep{zhang_topological_2009,guo_roadmap_2015,cortie_creating_2020,han_effect_2013,de_a_deus_magnetic_2021,checkelsky_trajectory_2014,haazen_ferromagnetism_2012,klimovskikh_tunable_2020,fu_electronic_2021,sun_sb2te3_2023,zhang_quantum_2021,Zhang2023TuningStudy}.

Our comparative framework isolates two orthogonal dimensions of complexity. The first involves the nature of the evaluated configurations, transitioning from thermal fluctuations to dopant migration and collective sliding. The second involves how these configurations are generated: static reference paths test fixed energy assignments, whereas MLIP-relaxed bands and MD trajectories probe the feedback loop between model predictions and subsequent phase-space evolution. This distinction clarifies the specific benefits of foundation initialization and target adaptation, while revealing where apparent improvements fail to transfer.

Here we systematically evaluate how foundation initialization, target-data adaptation, and specialist training perform across thermal, migrational, and sliding regimes within a single host system. By directly connecting static held-out accuracy with model-generated dynamics, we map process-specific strengths and failure modes across these distinct physical regimes. Rather than pursuing a single global ranking, this work establishes a process-aware validation protocol for selecting, fine-tuning, and deploying interatomic potentials based on target physical observables.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}
To determine how training lineage and initialization dictate model reliability, we evaluate specialist, zero-shot, and fine-tuned potentials across a hierarchy of physical processes in Cr-doped \ce{Sb2Te3}. We begin by examining pointwise energy and force accuracy on held-out test data, and subsequently test how these static metrics translate into dynamical stability, reaction-path fidelity, and collective deformation under out-of-equilibrium conditions.

\subsection{Pointwise accuracy gives ambiguous signals for energies and forces}\label{sec:pointwise}
Held-out evaluations show that energy and force metrics yield conflicting model rankings, highlighting the inadequacy of static scalar errors for predicting functional model suitability (Fig.~\ref{fig:parity}a--h). Fine-tuning consistently lowers test force errors relative to scratch training across both target-data regimes (Fig.~\ref{fig:parity}e--h), whereas energy errors follow a distinct, non-monotonic trend (Fig.~\ref{fig:parity}a--d). On the 600~K test partition, FT-600K achieves a lower force RMSE ($260\pm6$~meV·\AA$^{-1}$) than Scratch-600K ($344\pm36$~meV·\AA$^{-1}$), yet exhibits a higher energy RMSE ($41\pm5$ vs. $30\pm3$~meV·atom$^{-1}$; \sitab{tab:current_pointwise}). 

These results illustrate that optimizing force predictions does not guarantee a corresponding improvement in the energy landscape, nor does a lower static energy error ensure better force accuracy. Because these pointwise metrics offer ambiguous guidance on overall potential quality, process-aware evaluations across dynamical and out-of-equilibrium tasks become essential to determine downstream physical reliability.

\subsection{Trajectory survival does not guarantee local dynamical fidelity}\label{sec:md}
Trajectory survival differs sharply across the tested training routes (Fig.~\ref{fig:md}a--f). All three zero-shot foundation checkpoints successfully reach the 1~ns target. Among the adapted checkpoints, only FT-600K (seed 234) achieves this duration, whereas the remaining two FT-600K runs terminate at 236 and 485~ps, and FT-MultiT runs end between 237.2 and 286~ps. All six scratch models collapse at a scientific stopping threshold between 200.4 and 204.4~ps (\sitab{tab:md_horizons}). These trajectories demonstrate that zero-shot foundation checkpoints systematically sustain longer usable simulation horizons than adapted or specialist counterparts.

However, trajectory survival alone does not guarantee local dynamical accuracy. Despite exhibiting similar principal peaks in all-atom radial distribution functions (Fig.~\ref{fig:md}d,e), foundation trajectories display significantly higher in-plane mean-square displacement (MSD) mobility and distinct local Cr coordination environments (Fig.~\ref{fig:md}c). This divergence proves that global structural averages can readily obscure local dopant discrepancies. While kinetic temperature remains well-regulated across stable runs, hydrostatic pressure and other thermodynamic indicators exhibit pronounced model-dependent variations (Fig.~\ref{fig:md}a,b), highlighting that different training lineages sample non-equivalent physical ensembles even when trajectories persist.

Because premature trajectory truncation precludes standard long-time transport analysis, these comparative simulations are not interpreted as equilibrium diffusion coefficients. Instead, we analyze recorded MD trajectories strictly to benchmark dynamical stability, local coordination, and short-time mobility across model lineages.

\subsection{Model-relaxed migration pathways expose barrier collapse despite static energy accuracy}\label{sec:migration}

While MD reveals divergent local mobilities, isolating the energetic origins of these discrepancies requires probing specific transition pathways. To this end, we design a hierarchy of Cr migration probes: (1) in-gap diffusion (low distortion), (2) gap-to-QL entry (interfacial crossing), and (3) within-QL motion (strong local distortion) to untangle how foundation, fine-tuned, and specialist models navigate distinct physical terrains (Fig.~\ref{fig:profiles}a--f).

Migration energetics depend on both the spatial regime and whether evaluations use fixed reference geometries or unconstrained model-relaxed pathways. On fixed reference configurations, foundation and fine-tuned checkpoints reproduce the qualitative shape of the in-gap diffusion reference more accurately than legacy scratch models (Fig.~4a; Fig.~5b). However, this apparent transferability breaks down in the dense layer interior. For the within-QL trajectory, foundation and fine-tuned potentials assign excessive energy penalties, often exceeding the DFT reference by an order of magnitude, to strongly distorted intermediate configurations (Fig.~4c; Fig.~5c). In contrast, Scratch-600K achieves substantially smaller fixed-image discrepancies of $0.45\pm0.05$~eV (Supplementary Table S4). While this localized pointwise accuracy is reproducible across scratch initializations, these same models rapidly collapse during MD simulation, reinforcing that accurate static energy assignments are insufficient to construct a reliable global force field.

The discrepancies emerge when structural relaxation is driven entirely by the models' own forces. Unconstrained pathway relaxation unmasks unphysical deviations across all model lineages. Several MLIP-relaxed profiles exhibit spurious deep intermediate minima or suppressed activation barriers, entirely missing the reference topology (Fig.~4d--f). Notably, most within-QL forward peaks collapse toward zero despite their massive fixed-image energy overestimations (Fig.~5d). This barrier collapse results directly from unguided force-matching driving the system into unphysical local basins or altering the transition endpoint registry.

By benchmarking against fully converged, first-principles NEB reference pathways, we expose the uncoupling of static energy prediction and force-driven structural evolution. The inability of MLIPs to preserve validated activation barriers during self-relaxation underscores a methodological mandate: an optimizer-converged elastic band cannot support macroscopic kinetic interpretations without explicit structural and energetic validation against first-principles references.

\subsection{Collective sliding tests what local adaptation leaves unconstrained}\label{sec:sliding}

Relative layer displacement changes registry throughout the entire structure. It therefore probes a distinct response from moving a single Cr atom, even though both processes occur within the same host lattice. Figure~\ref{fig:taskmatrix}e and \sifig{fig:sliding_all}a--d compare pristine and Cr-containing sliding profiles. Predicted energy peaks and profile shapes exhibit pronounced variations across the foundation, fine-tuned, and scratch model lineages. While specific foundation and fine-tuned checkpoints approach the correct reference peak magnitude, they frequently fail to reproduce the full topological profile or the nominal endpoint energy return.

This divergence emerges prominently in the fixed-path curves. The DFT sliding profiles return perfectly to their initial reference energy after a full periodic displacement, whereas MLIPs across different training routes retain an artificial endpoint offset. An accurate peak magnitude can therefore coexist with an incorrect structural registry response. This persistent endpoint offset demonstrates that learning localized coordination environments through either massive zero-shot pre-training or targeted fine-tuning fails to intrinsically constrain the global periodic energy registry.

By integrating these sliding responses with the migration and MD evaluations (Fig.~\ref{fig:taskmatrix}), the combined results establish a strict decoupling of model capabilities. As demonstrated, a scratch training route achieving minimal static energy discrepancy collapses under its own MD. Conversely, a foundation checkpoint sustaining a stable MD trajectory severely misassigns local within-QL migration barriers. Finally, collective sliding imposes a periodic endpoint constraint that remains independent of these localized successes. Consequently, MD stability, local transition barriers, and collective sliding responses represent orthogonal dimensions of model reliability, dictating that no single performance metric can validate an MLIP for complex materials discovery.

\subsection{Trajectory projections diagnose divergence within shared structural manifolds}\label{sec:representations}

The orthogonal failure modes observed across MD, local migration, and collective sliding mandate a direct interrogation of the models' representational mechanisms. To determine whether these divergences stem from exploring completely isolated structural phases or from differing internal feature sensitivities, we first map the extrinsic phase space of the simulations. Trajectory projections visualize the phase space explored by the 15 model conditions during the MD runs (Fig.~\ref{fig:representation}a--c). These projected features incorporate recorded energies, forces, and structural summaries to capture the macroscopic evolution of each model. The resulting spatial separation visualizes distinct survival lifetimes and failure horizons, as the scratch trajectories terminate early and isolate their populations within the projected space.

Quantitative clustering metrics reveal the underlying structural relationship among these divergent trajectories. The unprojected output-feature silhouette is $6.3\times10^{-2}$, shifting to $-5.9\times10^{-2}$ after feature standardization, while the two-dimensional $t$-SNE projection yields $1.2\times10^{-1}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:representation}d; \sifig{fig:current_feature_audit}a). Repeated $t$-SNE evaluations across varying perplexities and training seeds bound the projected silhouettes between $7.9\times10^{-2}$ and $1.3\times10^{-1}$ (\sifig{fig:current_feature_audit}b). This pervasive low cluster separation persists across all representations, including raw model energies with distinct reference offsets, PCA-reduced SOAP summaries, and common 30-bin neighbor histograms (silhouette $5.6\times10^{-2}$ before standardization). 

These low silhouette metrics establish a specific physical mechanism. The divergent MD trajectories inhabit heavily overlapping local structural environments. Consequently, visual cluster separation reflects macroscopic trajectory divergence driven by cumulative force integration errors over time. This structural overlap mandates extracting fundamental model errors directly from explicit process observables. The projections identify macroscopic failure horizons, pointing to subtle force field inaccuracies that accumulate within a shared geometric phase space.

\subsection{Error surrogates expose the instability of learned feature associations}\label{sec:shap}

Because trajectory projections confirm that diverging models occupy shared structural environments, their kinetic failures must originate from how they internally map these geometries to predictive forces. Error surrogates evaluate this intrinsic mapping by ranking the learned feature associations. Feature rankings vary heavily across training seeds and model conditions (Fig.~\ref{fig:shap}a,b). Te--Te and Cr-containing descriptors emerge as the leading features for fine-tuned checkpoints, whereas scratch conditions emphasize distinct combinations of host and dopant environments. The between-seed variation observed across identical architectures confirms that models capture structural behavior through degenerate, distributed feature representations. This indicate the attribution of macroscopic migration fidelity to a single dominant causal channel.

The cross-validation diagnostic yields $R^2$ values of $0.98\pm3\times10^{-3}$ for FT-600K, $0.97\pm3\times10^{-3}$ for FT-MultiT, $0.98\pm6\times10^{-3}$ for Scratch-600K, and $0.95\pm2.2\times10^{-2}$ for Scratch-MultiT (\sitab{tab:current_shap_fidelity}). These metrics quantify the in-distribution fidelity of the five-fold diagnostic surrogate across the specific training seeds. Furthermore, the final higher-capacity TreeSHAP estimator operates on the full common sample to provide a closed-set mapping of the internal representations, establishing a localized interpretability baseline for the observed training distributions.

A supplementary coordinate-perturbation study evaluates the local relaxation response of native MACE inputs across six dopant--host combinations. This evaluation confirms the physical-input robustness of the models and establishes that macroscopic migration errors arise from complex, multi-channel interactions acting collectively across the structural descriptors.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

When does a foundation model help? In this study, the answer depends on the physical observable being tested. The zero-shot checkpoints sustain the longest MD trajectories, whereas target adaptation minimizes held-out force errors and improves specific fixed-image energy alignments. Neither benefit guarantees the other. Within-QL distortions expose massive energy overestimations in foundation and fine-tuned models, while scratch models exhibiting high static accuracy collapse rapidly during MD. The central lesson is clear: a transferable prior and a trustworthy physical simulation are entirely different achievements. A foundation model delivers practical value only when explicitly validated for the intended dynamic process.

These separated failure modes dictate a clear order for model validation. Candidate models must first demonstrate static accuracy against first-principles references across the specific coordination changes relevant to the process. Validation must then proceed to self-driven structural relaxations and short MD trajectories to directly test stability and convergence. Finally, collective displacements and endpoint-return checks impose necessary periodic constraints that local thermal sampling leaves untested. Success at any single stage validates only that specific regime, confirming that orthogonal observables require independent verification.

Distribution shift offers a useful framework for these failures, but its application requires a precise physical definition. Adaptation datasets routinely miss process-relevant transition geometries even within matching chemical and temperature ranges. Model-driven trajectories actively exacerbate this shift by exploring configurations absent from the reference set. Furthermore, discrepancies between pretraining and target electronic-structure protocols inject systemic calibration errors. Because foundation pretraining datasets are massive, researchers must isolate specific physical mismatches rather than relying on broad numerical distance metrics. Macroscopic model failure fundamentally arises from the interplay of these nested data shifts.

This targeted divergence provides a clear objective for active learning. Optimal data acquisition must focus on configurations where self-driven model rollouts expose unstable forces or unphysical topological profiles. Subsequent model updates demand rigorous evaluation against held-out dynamic tests alongside strict non-regression checks for previously stable trajectories. This feedback loop directly governs the choice between continued adaptation and specialist training. While feature attributions and ensemble disagreements can nominate candidate structures, their true acquisition value relies on proven performance gains under a fixed first-principles computational budget.

\rev{A4}{These findings establish a strict evaluation standard for MACE implementations in Cr-doped \ce{Sb2Te3}, providing a clear methodological blueprint for broader dopant-host systems and alternative neural architectures.} The topological sensitivity of the diagnostic pathways focuses macroscopic evaluations strictly on structural mechanisms, rather than predicting absolute kinetic rates. Ultimately, a foundation model helps when its zero-shot robustness withstands the specific dynamic process under investigation, and target adaptation helps only when localized static improvements translate reliably into stable macroscopic simulations.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The deployment of MLIPs in materials discovery exposes a disconnect between static accuracy and dynamic reliability. By evaluating foundation, fine-tuned, and scratch models across distinct physical tasks in Cr-doped \ce{Sb2Te3}, we demonstrate that these capabilities represent orthogonal dimensions of model performance. Models exhibiting high static fidelity on fixed-reference structures frequently collapse during unconstrained MD. Conversely, zero-shot foundation models that sustain prolonged dynamics severely mischaracterize localized transition barriers and collective periodic registries. Trajectory projections and feature attributions reveal the underlying physical mechanism for these divergences. Macroscopic model failures emerge from subtle force integration errors accumulating within shared geometric environments, driven by highly degenerate and unstable learned feature representations.

These findings establish a methodological mandate for the materials modeling community. The evaluation of universal priors and specialized potentials must advance beyond static benchmarking datasets. A reliable force field requires hierarchical validation that tests self-driven structural relaxations, transition pathway topologies, and macroscopic collective responses. Ultimately, AI-driven materials discovery demands process-specific dynamic validation. A transferable prior accelerates research only when its robustness withstands the precise physical process under investigation, ensuring that localized static accuracy translates reliably into faithful macroscopic simulations.

\subsection{Reference data and model lineages}\label{sec:models}
The reference calculations and AIMD data utilize PBE-based Quantum ESPRESSO for Cr-doped \ce{Sb2Te3}~\citep{giannozzi2009quantum,kohn_self-consistent_1965,perdew1996generalized}. The 600~K target pool exactly matches the thermostat temperature of the MD simulations. The MultiT pool broadens thermal coverage to test temperature transferability to the target trajectories and process configurations. Path-specific computational settings and convergence states are detailed in Supplementary Sec.~\ref{SI-sec:reference}.

The MD comparison evaluates the MPA-0, MP-0b3, and OMAT-0 zero-shot foundation checkpoints alongside three distinct seeds for each of four trained conditions: Scratch-600K, FT-600K, Scratch-MultiT, and FT-MultiT. Fine-tuned grouped-A2 models initialize from an OMAT-FT checkpoint; scratch models deploy the registered $L_{\max}=1$ architecture. Legacy pathway checkpoints appear separately in Fig.~\ref{fig:profiles}a--f and Fig.~\ref{fig:taskmatrix}b--e. Figure~\ref{fig:taskmatrix}a and \sifig{fig:training_design}a,b summarize the evaluation and training workflows.

\rev{C10}{Training, validation, and test partitions are split by complete source trajectories to prevent adjacent-frame leakage across data splits.} Within each temperature regime, scratch and fine-tuned models share identical target-data splits and use seeds 123, 234, and 345. The 600~K pair uses 1,786/311/267 training/validation/test configurations, while the MultiT pair uses 2,659 training and 767 test configurations. This design enables a direct evaluation of training routes at a fixed 600~K baseline and under broadened thermal coverage. \rev{A2}{An overlap audit of 25 legacy migration images confirms the absence of exact or threshold-defined SOAP near-duplicates within the audited training files.} \rev{A5}{The separate 89-configuration Scratch-5\% control serves as a data-volume sensitivity baseline.} The overall route comparison evaluates the integrated macroscopic effects of pretraining, architecture, and optimization. \rev{C8}{Finally, the force-supervised training protocol enforces a physical non-zero force requirement, admitting exactly zero forces exclusively for isolated-atom energy references.}

\subsection{Process observables and uncertainty}\label{sec:operators}
Fixed-path scoring evaluates every model on identical reference geometries, whereas self-relaxed scoring initializes an NEB path and evolves intermediate geometries driven by the model's own forces. These operators interrogate distinct physical properties, as self-relaxation simultaneously alters the sampled configurations and the resulting energy landscape. For a reference image set $\Gamma_{\rm ref}=\{X_i\}$, the fixed-path forward peak is:
\begin{equation}
B_M^{\rm fixed}(\Gamma_{\rm ref})=\max_i\{E_M(X_i)-E_M(X_0)\}.
\end{equation}
The diagnostic discrepancy $|B_M^{\rm fixed}-B_{\rm ref}|$ quantifies activation-energy errors strictly when the electronic reference, pathway optimization, and endpoint definitions are fully converged. Self-relaxed evaluations compute $\Gamma_M=\operatorname{NEB}_M(X_0,X_1)$ instead. Validating a self-relaxed physical barrier requires explicit structural correspondence alongside the peak energy. \rev{A1}{The fixed and self-relaxed operators independently measure supplied-path calibration and model-driven structural optimization; their respective peak energies characterize fundamentally distinct physical responses.}

\rev{C9}{Each MD run initiates from the identical 2,050-atom state at a simulation clock of 200~ps and targets a total simulated time of 1~ns.} The timestep is 1~fs, trajectory output occurs every 200 steps, the thermostat target is 600~K, the Langevin friction parameter is 0.01, and the MD seed is 1100. Trajectory durations are reconstructed directly from recorded steps, with stopped runs terminating precisely at their physical failure points. Sample standard deviations across training seeds quantify checkpoint initialization variance, while foundation model variation reflects differences across distinct pretrained architectures. 

\subsection{Diagnostic analysis and reproducibility}\label{sec:diagnostics}
The trajectory-feature analysis embeds 927 sampled frames from 15 MD model conditions using $t$-SNE, PHATE, and UMAP. \rev{C12}{Quantitative distances map macroscopic output features, with any constant-valued placeholders rigorously excluded prior to spatial evaluation.} Original-space scores are calculated before and after standardization, and $t$-SNE projections are repeated at perplexities 5, 30, and 50 with nominal seeds 0, 42, and 123. A common neighbour-histogram analysis ensures a shared physical basis across the divergent models, providing an objective structural comparison framework alongside the independently fitted SOAP PCA coordinates.

The SHAP analysis evaluates a separate, common 267-frame, 86-atom held-out trajectory across all four trained conditions and their respective seeds. The target metric is the absolute energy error per atom; inputs comprise 390 periodic SOAP features and 17 structural descriptors. A gradient-boosted regressor with 200 estimators, a learning rate of 0.05, a depth of 8, a subsampling rate of 0.8, and random state 42 supplies the full-sample TreeSHAP values. A separate 50-estimator, depth-4 surrogate utilizes shuffled five-fold validation to rigorously quantify in-distribution diagnostic fidelity across the sampled trajectory. Full definitions and per-seed metrics are detailed in Supplementary Sec.~\ref{SI-sec:shap}.
